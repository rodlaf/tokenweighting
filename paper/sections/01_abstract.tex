\begin{abstract}\noindent
Credit assignment remains a central difficulty in reinforcement learning for language models: outcome rewards arrive at the trajectory level, while policy updates are applied token by token. Learned critics are one response, but in language settings the prefix state is high-dimensional, semantically heterogeneous, and often a weak object for value estimation. We study a lighter alternative: \emph{intrinsic token weighting}, which redistributes a sequence-level advantage across tokens using signals already available from the policy, namely token surprisal and entropy reduction. Our contribution is intentionally narrow and modular: we do not estimate token rewards, train auxiliary process models, or construct search trees; instead, we treat token weighting as a drop-in credit-allocation prior for critic-free verifiable-reward RL. We evaluate the idea in a controlled synthetic benchmark suite of verifiable arithmetic and executable program-trace tasks, implemented as a fully reproducible NumPy reference stack. The results show task-dependent but consistent gains over uniform token updates: on arithmetic traces, surprisal weighting improves pass@4 from 29.2\% to 32.7\%, while on program-trace RL, entropy-reduction weighting improves greedy accuracy from 13.3\% to 23.3\% and pass@4 from 27.1\% to 43.4\%. The experiments suggest that intrinsic weighting can concentrate update mass on semantically important decisions without adding critics, token-level reward models, or rollout trees, making it a promising primitive for larger-scale RLVR systems.
\end{abstract}
