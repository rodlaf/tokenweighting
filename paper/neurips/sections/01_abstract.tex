\begin{abstract}\noindent Token-level credit assignment for language-model
reinforcement learning is an active area of research, with a rapidly growing
literature of methods that reweight or redistribute policy-gradient credit using
intrinsic signals such as surprisal, entropy, and divergence from a reference
policy. In parallel, nearly all practical LLM-RL pipelines rely on
parameter-efficient fine-tuning, most commonly LoRA. Yet these two threads are
studied in isolation: credit-assignment methods are developed and evaluated
under full-parameter training assumptions, while PEFT is treated as an
implementation detail orthogonal to the learning signal. We show this
separation is incorrect. Because LoRA constrains the policy to a small
neighbourhood of the reference, the \emph{per-token} differences between
policy and reference that drive intrinsic credit signals become approximately
position-uniform, collapsing surprisal-, entropy-reduction-, and
divergence-weighted updates to near-uniform broadcast. We provide a theoretical
characterization of this collapse, supported by a unified diagnostic
measurement (weight Gini and effective-token-count) across five weighting
schemes. We then propose \emph{Adapter-Residual Credit Assignment} (ARCA), a
credit-assignment mechanism that sidesteps the collapse by deriving per-token
salience directly from the adapter's own hidden-state residual
$\|h^{\text{adapted}}_t - h^{\text{base}}_t\|_2$, a signal that is guaranteed
to be non-degenerate whenever the adapter is non-trivially active. ARCA
requires no additional networks, reward models, or tree construction, and
reuses infrastructure already needed for KL-regularized LoRA RL. Experiments
on GSM8K with Qwen2.5 demonstrate that ARCA is the only intrinsic weighting
scheme that maintains non-degenerate per-token structure under LoRA, and
provides a concrete fix for the PEFT-credit interaction that no prior
token-level method addresses.
\end{abstract}
